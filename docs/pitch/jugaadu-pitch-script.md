# Jugaadu — Pitch Script

The script below is written for a ~3-minute spoken pitch (demo day / investor
meeting opener). A 30-second elevator version and Q&A ammo follow. Stage
directions are in *italics*. Fill in the bracketed numbers with live stats
before you go on stage — never say a bracket out loud.

---

## How to pitch it (read this first)

1. **Lead with the unfairness, not the product.** The hook is "jobs get filled
   before they're posted." Everyone in the room has either felt that or hired
   that way. Land it, pause, then introduce Jugaadu as the fix.
2. **Sell the outcome, not the agents.** "Four agents" is architecture. "Paste
   a job link, and twenty minutes later a tailored resume and a verified email
   to the actual hiring manager are sitting in your Desk" is a pitch.
3. **One demo beat, end to end.** Don't tour the tabs. One input → one
   finished, sendable output. The blur-gate moment ("the work is done, it's
   waiting behind the blur") is your best screenshot.
4. **Own the name.** Jugaad — the clever, resourceful hack — is the whole
   thesis: the system is rigged toward insiders, so give everyone else an
   insider's move. Say it with pride; it's the most memorable thing on stage.
5. **Memorize exactly three numbers.** Your live user/run count, one outcome
   stat (replies or interviews), and the market number. Everything else can
   be approximate.

## Main script (~3 minutes)

### 1. Hook — the rigged line (0:00–0:30)

*Walk out. No slide, or a black slide. Slow first line.*

> Raise your hand if you've ever applied to a job online and heard nothing
> back. *(pause)* Keep it up if you later found out someone got that job
> through a referral.
>
> That's not bad luck. That's how hiring works. The majority of good roles
> are filled through referrals — often before the posting is even public.
> There are two lines into every company: the front door, where a thousand
> resumes go to die in an ATS, and the side door, where someone who knows
> the hiring manager walks in. Almost everyone is stuck in the wrong line.

### 2. Problem (0:30–1:00)

> Getting into the right line is a full-time job. You have to rewrite your
> resume for every posting, figure out who on the team actually decides,
> hunt down an email address that isn't a guess, and write a cold message
> that doesn't sound like a template — and then remember to follow up. Most
> people don't have twenty hours a week for that. So they spray, they pray,
> and the ATS eats them alive.

### 3. Solution — the crew (1:00–1:45)

*Switch to the product. This is the demo beat.*

> This is Jugaadu. Jugaad is the Hindi word for the clever hack — the
> resourceful move that beats a rigged system. Jugaadu is a crew of AI
> agents that runs the side-door play for you, end to end.
>
> You paste a job link — or just a screenshot of the posting. Then the crew
> goes to work. One agent rewrites your resume against that exact JD and
> clears ATS — PDF and Word, ready to send. Another scrapes the team, ranks
> everyone by fit, and locks in the one person who actually decides. A third
> verifies their real email — Apollo, Hunter, MX checks — until it's an
> address, not a guess. And a fourth reads what that person has been posting,
> finds a genuine hook, and drafts the outreach in *your* voice — email,
> LinkedIn, and a follow-up already queued.
>
> One paste. Twenty minutes. You go from "applicant #847" to a verified
> email in the hiring manager's inbox with a resume built for that role.

### 4. Why it works / why now (1:45–2:15)

> Two things make this possible now. Agents finally got good enough to do
> real multi-step work — research, verify, draft — not just chat. And the
> front door got worse: companies now use AI to screen you, so the only
> resumes that survive are the ones an insider carries past the pile.
>
> And Jugaadu compounds. It keeps your Story — everything you've shipped —
> so every resume gets sharper and every draft sounds more like you, not
> less. This isn't a resume builder or a job board. Those optimize the
> wrong line. We skip the line.

### 5. Traction (2:15–2:35)

*Swap in real numbers before stage. Cut anything you can't back.*

> We're live at jugaadu.app, free in beta, built in public. [X] users have
> run [Y] crews. [Outcome stat — replies received / interviews landed / "the
> first user to send a crew-drafted email got a reply from the hiring
> manager in N hours."]

### 6. Vision + close (2:35–3:00)

> The job hunt is the wedge, because it's the moment people are most
> desperate for leverage. But the crew doesn't stop when you're hired. Next
> agents publish your essays, brief your mornings, drill your next skill,
> and map your network — a personal OS for ambition. Eight agents. One you.
>
> The ambitious used to hire assistants, publicists, coaches. Now everyone
> can. Don't be average — be jugaadu.

*Hold. Don't add a "thank you" ramble — one nod, done.*

## Q&A ammo

**"Isn't cold outreach spam? Won't this flood hiring managers?"**
The opposite of spam is what we build: one researched person, a real hook
from their own posts, in the sender's actual voice, with a verified address.
And there's a human in the loop — the crew drafts and hands off; the user
sends. Spray-and-pray is what people do *without* Jugaadu.

**"What stops OpenAI / LinkedIn from doing this?"**
LinkedIn monetizes recruiters and job ads — helping candidates route around
postings cannibalizes their business. General assistants won't do the ugly
integration work: Apollo lookups, MX verification, ATS scoring, follow-up
state. We win on the workflow and on the Story — the personal memory that
makes output sound like you.

**"How do you make money?"**
Free beta now to earn the outcome stories. Then subscription per agent or
per crew — this audience already pays for resume reviews, coaches, and
LinkedIn Premium, for far less leverage. First 250 users keep three agents
free forever, which is our built-in referral engine.

**"Why the Indian branding?"**
Because jugaad *is* the product thesis, and because the most motivated job
seekers on earth — international students, H-1B holders, ambitious
immigrants — feel the rigged line hardest. The brand is a filter for exactly
the users who will love us. It travels: everyone everywhere knows the
system favors insiders.

**"What's defensible?"**
The Story. Every run enriches a personal corpus of what you've shipped and
how you write. Month three's drafts are better than day one's, and none of
it ports to a competitor.

**"Emails/data — is the sourcing okay?"**
We use commercial data providers (Apollo, Hunter) under their terms, verify
rather than scrape-and-guess, and the user sends from their own account —
we never send on their behalf.
